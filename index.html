01 / Stellar Evolution

Supernovas

When a star exceeding eight solar masses exhausts its nuclear fuel, its core can no longer withstand the relentless inward pull of gravity. Within a fraction of a second, the core collapses at nearly a quarter the speed of light.

The resulting shockwave rebounds outward, blasting the star's outer layers into interstellar space. In that solitary instant, the supernova outshines entire galaxies and forges the universe's heaviest elements.

02 / Relativistic Gravity

Spaghettification

Near a stellar-mass black hole, gravity changes with extreme rapidity over tiny distances. The gravitational pull on an object's nearest edge is astronomically greater than on its farthest edge.

This extreme tidal differential stretches any matter vertically into an ultra-thin strand while compressing it horizontally. The formal astrophysics term for this vertical stretching is Spaghettification.

03 / Theoretical Physics

The Cosmic Web & Multiverse

From cosmic inflation to dark matter filaments, current cosmology suggests our visible universe may be just one membrane in an infinite ensemble.

LEVEL I
Infinite Hubble Volumes
In an infinite, flat universe, matter must eventually repeat configurations. Far beyond our particle horizon, identical regions of space inevitably exist.
LEVEL II
Eternal Inflation
Cosmic inflation doesn't stop everywhere at once. It continuously seeds isolated bubble universes, each potentially with different physical constants.
LEVEL III
Many-Worlds Hypothesis
In quantum mechanics, every quantum measurement branches reality into parallel, non-communicating branches of a universal wavefunction.
